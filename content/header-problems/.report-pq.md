## What every grader in this topic does

One shared design, adapted per problem (each `grader_main.cpp` is
self-contained):

- **a command stream**, never a fixed input shape: the target is interleaved
  with `push` / `pop` / `top` / `size` / copy-construction / copy-assignment;
- **four instantiations per problem**, two of them with a non-default `Comp`:
  `int/std::less`, `int/std::greater` (a min-heap), `long long/std::less`, and
  `Item/ItemOrder` — a struct with **no `operator<` and no `operator==`**, so a
  submission that compares with `<` or `==` instead of `mLess` does not even
  compile for those blocks (Compilation Error, 0%), while the min-heap blocks
  catch the ones that compile but assume numeric order;
- **`heap_ok()`**, a pre-implemented member injected through `declarations`
  (only the grader calls it), which checks the heap property directly — so for
  the mutating targets (`update`, `erase`, `merge`) a right answer on top of a
  broken invariant is caught at once, not by luck;
- **a block epilogue** that re-checks the heap property and drains the heap in
  priority order, hashing what comes out, so the whole heap is verified rather
  than the lines that were printed;
- **procedural data**: a `fill c seed span` command pushes `c` generated
  elements, and `burst` commands run thousands of queries from one input line.
  That is how heaps of 10⁶ elements and bursts of 2·10⁵ queries fit in 31–890
  KiB of testcase data per problem (budget: ~6 MB).

Equally important for the per-problem notes below: every `Comp` used is a
**total order** on the data (two elements of equal priority are identical
values), so an implementation that may legitimately pick either of two "equal"
elements still produces the same output.

## Per problem

### 1. `hp_pq01_min` — difficulty 1400 — `const T &min() const`

- **Target** the LEAST important element, O(n) accepted, `std::out_of_range`
  when empty. Pre-implemented on top of it: `all_equal()`, which compares
  `top()` with the student's `min()`.
- **Catalogue deviation — see "Deviations" below:** the catalogue asks for
  `top()`, which cannot be written as a header problem (it is already defined
  in `lib/priority_queue.h`).
- **Grader stresses** min-heaps and the `Item` order (scanning with `<` fails
  or does not compile), the empty-heap throw, `all_equal` on heaps that are
  all one value, and interleaved push/pop so the answer moves.
- **Subtasks** 2/20% (≤ 20 elements, int) · 3/30% (≤ 400, adds min-heap,
  `long long`, `Item`) · 5/50% (≤ 20,000 elements, thousands of `min()` calls).
- **Measured** reference 41 ms of 1000 ms, peak 13 MB of 256 MB; 232 KiB of
  testcase data. No TLE subtask (`min()` is Ω(n) however it is written; the
  statement says so and the cases bound elements × calls instead).
- **Mutation test** `return mData[mSize - 1]` ("the weakest one must be the
  last one pushed") → **Wrong Answer on case 1**, 0%. A variant that compares
  with `std::less<T>` instead of `mLess` fails to compile for the `Item`
  blocks.

### 2. `hp_pq02_is_heap` — difficulty 1600 — `bool is_heap() const`

- **Target** validate the heap property over `mData[0..mSize-1]`.
  `declarations` also injects `raw_push()` (append without `fixUp`) and
  `raw_swap()` (exchange two slots, repairing nothing) — the grader needs them
  because `mData` is protected, and they are what lets main build the
  "deliberately corrupted array" the catalogue asks for.
- **Grader stresses** roughly half the queries are about a damaged array; the
  damage is often a `raw_swap` of two slots, which leaves the multiset intact,
  so counting or looking only at the root cannot tell. Push/pop continue on
  top of the damaged array and must still be handled.
- **Subtasks** 3/30% (≤ 30 elements, int) · 3/30% (≤ 900, all four
  instantiations) · 4/40% (≤ 30,000, thousands of queries).
- **Measured** reference 10 ms of 1000 ms, peak 13 MB; 158 KiB. No TLE
  subtask (validation is Θ(n) by nature).
- **Mutation test** looping over parents and checking only the left child
  `2i+1` → **Wrong Answer on case 2**, 10%.

### 3. `hp_pq03_second` — difficulty 1800 — `T second() const`

- **Target** the second most important element in **O(1)**: it must be a child
  of the root, so the candidates are indices 1 and 2. Pre-implemented
  `take_two()` returns it and pops twice, so a wrong `second()` changes what
  the heap holds from then on.
- **Grader stresses** `mSize == 2` (index 2 does not exist yet), ties
  everywhere, the fewer-than-two throw, and `take_two` mixed into the stream.
- **Subtasks** 5/50% (≤ 2,000 elements — a scan still passes) · 5/50%
  (≤ 120,000 elements, ~10,000 `second()` calls).
- **Measured** reference 89 ms of 1000 ms, peak 14 MB; 886 KiB. `slow.h`
  (two passes over the array) 86 ms on case 5, **TLE on case 6** (1013 ms),
  and **7.0×** the limit on case 10.
- **Mutation test** `return mData[1]` (the left child, no comparison with
  `mData[2]`) → **Wrong Answer on case 3**, 20%.

### 4. `hp_pq04_third` — difficulty 2000 — `T third() const`

- **Target** the third most important element in **O(1)**: with `s` the more
  important root child (the second) and `o` the other one, the candidates are
  `o`, `mData[2s+1]`, `mData[2s+2]`. Pre-implemented `take_three()`.
- **Grader stresses** several blocks draw values from spans as narrow as
  ±3, so ties between the root's children are common and the candidate set has
  to be reasoned about; small `mSize` where `2s+1`/`2s+2` do not exist; the
  fewer-than-three throw.
- **Subtasks** 5/50% (≤ 2,000) · 5/50% (≤ 120,000, ~10,000 calls).
- **Measured** reference 81 ms of 1000 ms, peak 14 MB; 862 KiB. `slow.h`
  (three passes) 111 ms on case 5, **TLE on case 6** (1012 ms), **10.4×** the
  limit on case 10.
- **Mutation test** candidate set taken as indices 1..4 (the root's children
  plus the children of index 1, whichever child is actually the second) →
  **Wrong Answer on case 4**, 30%.

### 5. `hp_pq05_rank` — difficulty 2200 — `size_t rank(size_t i) const`

- **Target** how many elements are **strictly** more important than
  `mData[i]`; `std::out_of_range` for `i >= mSize`. Pre-implemented
  `is_top(i)` = `rank(i) == 0`.
- **Grader stresses** the whole problem is the strictness and the operand
  order of `mLess`: values come from spans of ±7 to ±25, so most queries have
  ties. `is_top` is asked all over the array (with ties, several slots answer
  1). Out-of-range indices are asked on purpose.
- **Subtasks** 3/30% (≤ 40, int) · 3/30% (≤ 1,200, all four instantiations) ·
  4/40% (≤ 20,000, hundreds of queries, heavy duplication).
- **Measured** reference 68 ms of 1000 ms, peak 14 MB; 132 KiB. No TLE
  subtask: the answer can be `mSize - 1`, so Ω(n) is unavoidable and the
  statement accepts O(n).
- **Mutation test** `!mLess(mData[j], mData[i])` instead of
  `mLess(mData[i], mData[j])` (counting equal elements as "more important") →
  **Wrong Answer on case 3**, 20%.

### 6. `hp_pq06_descendants` — difficulty 2400 — `size_t descendants(size_t i) const`

- **Target** the number of descendants of index `i` in **O(log n)**: the
  descendants at each level form a contiguous index range
  (`left = 2·left+1`, `right = 2·right+2`), clipped at `mSize - 1` on the last
  level. Pre-implemented `is_leaf(i)`.
- **Grader stresses** `burst` asks up to 1.8·10⁵ queries from one line with
  indices **near the root** (`i < 2..4`), which is where a subtree walk has to
  touch most of the array; the heap grows and shrinks between bursts so
  answers cannot be memorised; out-of-range indices must throw.
- **Subtasks** 5/50% (≤ 2,000 elements — a subtree walk still passes) ·
  5/50% (up to 10⁶ elements, same query counts).
- **Measured** reference 74 ms of 1000 ms, peak 15 MB; **51 KiB** of testcase
  data for 10⁶-element heaps. `slow.h` (subtree walk) 194 ms on case 5,
  **TLE on case 6** (1010 ms), **more than 15×** the limit on case 10.
- **Mutation test** counting each level as a full level (1, 2, 4, 8, …) and
  stopping when the leftmost descendant falls off the array — exact for
  perfect subtrees, overcounting otherwise → **Wrong Answer on case 1**, 0%.

### 7. `hp_pq07_update` — difficulty 2600 — `void update(size_t i, const T &value)`

- **Target** write the value and repair in **O(log n)**; one comparison
  against the parent decides `fixUp` or `fixDown`; `std::out_of_range` for
  `i >= mSize` with nothing modified.
- **Grader stresses** `update` prints nothing, so everything is caught later:
  after each `burst` of thousands of generated updates the grader prints the
  size, the top and `heap_ok()`, and the block ends with the drain. Values come
  from the elements' own span so about half the updates must sift up and half
  down; narrow-span blocks put equally important parents and children next to
  each other, which separates a strict comparison from a non-strict one.
- **Subtasks** 5/50% (≤ 20,000 elements — rebuilding the heap per update still
  passes) · 5/50% (up to 300,000 elements, >5,000 updates per block).
- **Measured** reference 51 ms of 1000 ms, peak 14 MB; 67 KiB. `slow.h`
  233 ms on case 5, **TLE on case 6** (1012 ms), **6.3×** on case 10.
- **`slow.h` note** it takes the cheap `fixUp` path when the element has to
  climb and rebuilds the whole heap bottom-up when it has to sink (Θ(n) per
  update, which is most of them). That shape is deliberate: this grader
  addresses `update` **by index**, so a naive version that rearranged the heap
  differently would be *wrong* rather than slow and would prove nothing. Every
  `fixDown` in the rebuild is either the one real sink at `i` or a no-op that
  at most exchanges elements of equal priority — and equal priority means an
  identical value here — so the array of values comes out exactly as the
  reference leaves it.
- **Mutation test** `mData[i] = value; fixUp(i);` (one direction only) →
  **Wrong Answer on case 2**, 10%.

### 8. `hp_pq08_erase` — difficulty 2800 — `void erase(const T &value)`

- **Target** remove one element of equal priority (or leave the heap alone if
  there is none), then repair. Three traps: "same value" must be written as
  `!mLess(a,b) && !mLess(b,a)` because `T` may have neither `==` nor `<`; the
  element that fills the hole comes from the end of the array and may have to
  climb **or** sink; and erasing the last slot must not repair anything
  (`mData[mSize]` is outside the live array).
- **Grader stresses** narrow-span blocks where nearly every erase hits and
  duplicates are everywhere, wide-span blocks where nearly every erase misses
  and must not touch the heap, plus `erase_top` (a **copy** of `top()`, which
  is the value `erase` is about to overwrite) and bursts of generated erases
  followed by `heap_ok()`.
- **Subtasks** 2/20% (≤ 60, int) · 3/30% (≤ 1,500, all four instantiations) ·
  5/50% (≤ 20,000, hundreds of erases).
- **Measured** reference 59 ms of 1000 ms, peak 14 MB; 88 KiB. No TLE
  subtask: locating the value is Ω(n) however the repair is written.
- **Mutation test** filling the hole and calling only `fixDown` →
  **Wrong Answer on case 3**, 20%. (Cases 3 and up are deliberately a few
  hundred elements tall for exactly this: on a shallow heap the element that
  fills the hole almost never needs to climb.)

### 9. `hp_pq09_merge` — difficulty 3100 — `void merge(priority_queue &other)`

- **Target** absorb `other` in **O(n+m)**: one `ensureCapacity`, append its
  array verbatim, then build a heap bottom-up with `fixDown` from the last
  parent back to index 0, and empty `other`.
- **Grader stresses** every merge prints the size of **both** heaps,
  `heap_ok()` and the top, so forgetting to empty `other` or leaving the array
  un-heapified is caught on the spot. `merge_list` covers the odd shapes
  (0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13 elements, operands bigger than the destination) and
  `merge_empty` covers the empty operand, which must read nothing. The heavy
  blocks build one template heap of 262,144 elements and merge a fresh copy of
  it 125–138 times, so what is measured is the merge and not the building of
  its operands.
- **Subtasks** 5/50% (≤ ~1.7 M elements merged per case — push-by-push still
  passes) · 5/50% (262,144-element merges repeated 125–138 times, values
  almost all identical).
- **Measured** reference 510 ms of **1500 ms** (see deviations), peak 14 MB;
  31 KiB. `slow.h` (m pushes) 119 ms on case 5, **TLE on case 6** (1505 ms),
  **1.8×** the limit on case 10.
- **Why the data looks the way it does** with random values a push-based merge
  is O(1) amortised per element and there is no gap at all. The templates
  therefore use a value span of 1 (identical elements) against a destination
  of span 2: `fixUp` stops only when the parent is **strictly** more
  important, so every naive push climbs the full log₂(n+m) levels, while
  bottom-up building stays O(n+m). Even so this is the thinnest gap in the
  topic — see "Unresolved".
- **Mutation test** the same merge with the `fixDown` sweep running from index
  0 upwards instead of from the last parent down → **Wrong Answer on case 1**,
  0%.

### 10. `hp_pq10_kth` — difficulty 3500 — `T kth(size_t k) const`

- **Target** the k-th most important element (1-indexed) in **O(k log k)**,
  heap unchanged, `std::out_of_range` for `k == 0` and `k > mSize`. The
  intended solution grows a frontier of candidate indices in a hand-written
  auxiliary heap ordered by `mLess(mData[a], mData[b])`; the statement
  explicitly lifts rule 6 for a local `new`/`delete` array of O(k) indices.
- **Grader stresses** bursts of thousands of queries with **small k** on heaps
  of up to 450,000 elements — the shape that kills everything proportional to
  `mSize`, including the tempting "copy the heap and pop k times"
  (O(n + k log n)) as well as sorting a copy; a few queries with k up to 10⁵
  to catch an O(k²) candidate set; `k = 0` and oversized k for the throw; and
  size/top/`heap_ok()` after every burst plus the final drain, since `kth`
  must not disturb the heap.
- **Subtasks** 5/50% (≤ 2,000 elements — sorting a copy per query still
  passes) · 5/50% (250,000–450,000 elements, >4,000 queries per block).
- **Measured** reference 92 ms of 1000 ms, peak 15 MB; 31 KiB. `slow.h`
  (copy + hand-written heapsort per query) 298 ms on case 5, **TLE on case 6**
  (1012 ms), **more than 15×** the limit on case 10.
- **Mutation test** offering only the left child of each extracted candidate
  to the frontier → **Wrong Answer on case 1**, 0%.

## Deviations from CATALOGUE.md

1. **`hp_pq01` target renamed from `top()` to `min()`.** The catalogue asks the
   student to write `const T &top() const`, but `lib/priority_queue.h` already
   defines `top()` inline in the class, and `declarations` can only *add*
   members — a second declaration is a hard compile error (verified:
   "`const T& …::top() const` cannot be overloaded with …"). Since I may not
   edit `lib/`, the problem had to change rather than the library. Asking for
   a `max()` alias of the existing `top()` would have been a one-line
   non-problem, so the target is the mirror image — the least important
   element — which keeps the catalogue's intent (an O(1)/one-pass accessor,
   `push`/`pop` interleaved, a non-default `Comp` that breaks `<`) and adds a
   real idea: the minimum of a heap must be a leaf, so only `mSize/2 …
   mSize-1` need looking at.
2. **No "editable pre-existing function" in this topic.** The same
   redeclaration rule blocks moving `push`, `pop`, `top`, `fixUp`, `fixDown`,
   `expand` or `ensureCapacity` into `student.h` for *any* of the ten, so the
   device the stack/queue topics use is unavailable here. Instead every
   problem injects a small member **written on top of the student's function**
   (`all_equal`, `take_two`, `take_three`, `is_top`, `is_leaf`) plus
   `heap_ok()` for the grader: a wrong target therefore also breaks a function
   the student did not write, and for the mutating targets the invariant is
   checked directly.
3. **`hp_pq09_merge` runs at 1500 ms**, not the default 1000 ms. The honest
   separation between the intended O(n+m) build and an O(m log(n+m))
   push-by-push merge is only about 5–6× in measured time (the reference does
   ~3 linear passes where the naive version does log₂ m element moves per
   element), so the reference has to sit at roughly a third of the limit for
   the naive version to be comfortably over it. 1500 ms keeps the reference at
   510 ms (34%) and the naive at 1.8× the limit; at 1000 ms the same ratio put
   the naive at only 1.35×.
4. **TLE subtasks on three problems the catalogue does not mark.** The
   catalogue flags complexity subtasks only for `descendants`, `merge` and
   `kth`. `second`, `third` and `update` promise O(1)/O(log n) in their
   statements and have genuine gaps, so they got real TLE subtasks too (the
   statements say what a scan or a rebuild scores). `min`, `is_heap`, `rank`
   and `erase` are `has_tle_subtask: false`, each for a stated reason (Ω(n) is
   unavoidable in all four).
5. **`hp_pq08_erase` subtask split is 2/3/5**, not an even 3/3/4: the cases
   from 3 on need heaps a few hundred elements tall before a one-directional
   repair of the hole shows up at all, so the "tiny" subtask is two cases.
6. **`hp_pq02`'s corrupted array** is built through injected `raw_push()` /
   `raw_swap()` members rather than by main touching `mData`, which is
   protected and unreachable from the grader.

## Unresolved / worth knowing

- **`hp_pq09_merge` has the thinnest margin in the topic**: `slow.h` is 1.8×
  the limit on case 10 and, by construction, just over it at the subtask
  boundary (case 6). The ratio is bounded by log₂(m) divided by the three
  linear passes the reference makes, so it cannot be widened much by more
  data; if the production judge turns out to be materially faster per element
  than this build box, a push-by-push merge could creep into case 6 and score
  60% instead of 50%. Raising `time_limit_ms` further does not help (the
  constraint is the ratio, not the absolute time) — what would help is
  dropping the per-merge `heap_ok()` from the grader to cut the reference's
  own cost, at the price of a weaker invariant check. I kept the check.
- **Timing noise on this machine.** Other authoring agents were building in
  Docker throughout, and the same bytes measured 256 ms and 624 ms for
  `hp_pq09_merge`'s reference in two runs minutes apart. The numbers above are
  from the quieter runs, and the `--measure` figures were all re-confirmed in
  the final 10/10 pass. Anything calibrated near 40% of its limit (only pq09)
  deserves a second look on the real judge.
- **Mutants on `hp_pq03`/`hp_pq04` score 20–30%**, not 0%: cases 1–2 are only
  a few dozen commands, and `mData[1]` really is the second most important
  element most of the time on a tiny heap. That is partial credit working as
  intended rather than a hole, but if the course wants those bugs to score 0 the
  fix is to add ties to cases 1–2 (as `hp_pq05`'s generator does: it draws int
  elements from ±7 from case 1 on).
- **`hp_pq07`'s and `hp_pq09`'s `slow.h`** are documented in their own headers
  as being arrangement-compatible with the reference on purpose; a future
  editor who "simplifies" `hp_pq07`'s `slow.h` into a plain full rebuild will
  turn it from slow into wrong, because that grader updates by index.
